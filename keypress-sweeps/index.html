Building Three Keypress Sweeps for TUI Testing 

Jason Fullner
CycleVault · jsfullner@cyclevault.ai
Version 1.0 · 20 October 2026

I use a keypress sweep as part of testing and review. All reachable endpoints, popups, states and configurations can be produced repeatably with asciinema (asciinema.org, the open-source terminal recorder by Marcin Kulik). All keyboard keys are pressed and results recorded (from the terminal output of asciinema), covering each view, identifying stray bindings, or features hidden from the user. One .png is generated from the terminal output of asciinema per view. These outputs are compared with mockups that are fast to design and iterate upon. 

This is the result of prompting three software factories to build the complete keypress suite, capture all endpoints, record what every key does. FACTORY1 is the tutorial build of GasCity from Software Factory Intensive October 15. PLASMA is my own pre-seminar creation. Two of these factories used opus-5.5 orchestrators and deepseek-flash for all other roles. The result of studying those factories prompted me to port some of PLASMA's practices into GasCity. PLASMA-CITY is that build, assigning deepseek-flash as mayor of the lab's own Gas City install on a MacBook Air, with worker pools on three "classic" Dell office PCs. During this experiment, PLASMA-CITY received instructions relayed through another agent. The intent was to match the original FACTORY1 prompt as near as possible. The result was that the beads written by the mayor were exactly the two prompts sent to the other mayors as design guidance, sent as beads. This report compares how the factories produced their output, what went wrong, what it cost, and how long it took.

How the runs differed

Method

plasmafactory1plasma-city
Work orderplanner-write (opus-5.5) wrote a lane brief from plasma's template: a header (batch, host, gate, budget); the rules every lane reads (COMMON.md and lessons from earlier lanes); Why; Premises the planner checked at the start commit; numbered Pieces; Done (a REPORT.md with at least one real run); and Not in this lane. The batch adds a Done means check for the merger.The mayor (opus-5.5) wrote a bead: the deliverable and the base commit; the skill to read first and the engine to run; numbered steps (write the sweep config with its required settings, run the sweep detached and poll it, triage every flag in REVIEW.md, commit); and a Done when check (one full run, one PNG per screen, every flag triaged, no forbidden terms).The mayor (deepseek-flash) wrote a bead verbatim of the two instructions I had sent to the factory1 mayor. I asked an outside Claude agent to send the exact prompts that factory1 received. They sent that along with instructions to carry my words exactly and they did. Bead was not properly formed.

Stumbles

plasma

  • The lane waited 2 h 10 min for compute to become available.
  • The first run's engine restarted the app for every key. After 69 minutes it had swept 11 of 47 screens; it was stopped, and with no checkpoint its key table was lost.
  • The engine was rebuilt to press keys in sequence in one session (its test program went from 208.8 s to 31.4 s with identical results).
  • The rerun on the new engine was stopped by decision at 36 screens. A resume, triggered manually, was discarded.
  • The finish went back through plasma. The first resume lane exited in 3 seconds: the Mac lane launcher it calls had never been installed. Fixed and resent.
  • The second resume lane started from the checkpoint. Its fresh worktree lacked an exercises track the first leg had, so it found 72 screens; it restored the track, re-ran, finished at 73, and triaged all 172 flags.
  • 22 timeout flags show a flaw in design: after T starts a timer, the app repaints every 100 ms, so the screen never settles.

factory1

  • The first sweep ran as a background job of the builder's shell. When the session was recycled, the sweep died with it at 66 screens, and the engine had saved nothing. 16 minutes lost.
  • The engine was fixed mid-run (checkpoints, resume, detaching), and the sweep restarted and finished in 13.8 minutes.
  • A session restart released the implement step; the mayor pinned it back to the builder.
  • The pre-flight checks found a failure already on the main branch and filed it as its own work item, as the procedure requires.
  • The refinery recorded its approval 5 seconds after opening the pull request, not before: a known ordering bug in the gate.

plasma-city

  • The city's bead store hit a read error and its controller stalled for about an hour. Session Host on the Dells stops any session nobody has listened to for 5 minutes. The computer thread restarted the store and woke the session, it then re-read its own files and swept the last 6 endpoints.
  • The first merge judge returned the change: the documented incremental re-run wiped the full report (1,274 frames down to 2), the check for a quit without confirmation could never fire, and some counts and table rows were wrong. A fix worker repaired it in 8 minutes.
  • Nothing started the next merge rounds. The city's auto-merge only picks up branches named after the bead, and the worker had named its own, so fixed work waited 16 mins. and then 55 mins, until it was noticed and corrected through the mayor, which started each round by hand.
  • The second judge returned it because of a path prefix. The second fix took 6 minutes, and the third judge merged it.
  • The worker's model cannot read images, so it evaluated the sweep from the text twin of each frame.

Orchestrator cost

* The opus-5.5 orchestrators run on a Claude plan that is metered in percent of weekly usage. That cost is plan usage priced at $0.25 per 1%. The conversion from tokens to percent was measured from plasma's status-line log: 4,104 samples over 70 sessions (3 to 16 October), each holding the weekly and five-hour usage % and the session's running API-equivalent cost. Cost was calculated by token count, usage % was used to determine token cost for conversion.

Notes

  • Counted: from the request to the end of each run, including tooling built for it: plasma's first engine, factory1's sweep pack and smoke sweep. factory1 and plasma got the request at 17:50Z on 10-15; plasma-city got it at 22:38:00Z on 10-19, when its mayor read it, and is counted to its merge at 02:02:19Z on 10-20. Plasma's merge gate ran a day later, outside plasma: on 17 October, after plasma was retired, a plasma-city worker merged its sweep lanes and ran the gate (passed), and the batch landed on Recit's main at 22:26Z; its 66 calls ($0.045) are counted in plasma's figures. Other work in those hours is left out, separated by keyword, so a call can be misclassed. For plasma-city, the mayor's handling of an unrelated chat-page merge in the same turn is left out, and 73 calls that served both are split in proportion (53 to the sweep); the recitfactory rig's idle worker on dell-1, started and drained about once a minute, is left out.
  • Sources: per-call usage from each agent's session log and the mayor's transcript; times from each factory's event log. Factory1's logs were copied off its VM before shutdown. For plasma-city, the city's per-call usage log, every call re-priced at the list price below.
  • Prices: deepseek-flash at off-peak list price per million tokens: $0.15 input, $0.003 cached input, $0.60 output. DeepSeek doubles its prices in peak hours (01:00–04:00 and 06:00–10:00 UTC, Monday to Friday), and calls in them are priced double: plasma's last 25 calls (06:00–06:26Z on 10-16) and plasma-city's calls after 01:00Z on 10-20. The opus-5.5 orchestrators are plan usage at $0.25 per 1% weekly (see * Orchestrator cost), a floor; fable-5.1 calls use opus-5.5's conversion rate. plasma-city's orchestrator is a deepseek-flash mayor, priced at the list price above.
  • Not equal starts: all three are timed from the request. factory1's mayor (opus-5.5) wrote the sweep skill and its engine itself and smoke-tested them through the workers before the full sweep; plasma built its first engine in two lanes before queueing the sweep; both are in their factory's time and cost (factory1: $0.16 in workers plus 159 orchestrator calls, 1.57%, $0.39; plasma: $0.28 in workers). plasma-city's worker built its skill and engine inside the run. factory1's and plasma's orchestrators wrote work orders; plasma-city's mayor pasted the request into the bead with the fence, as the computer thread had told it to, and wrote no instructions. Terminal sizes differ (120 x 40 for factory1, 136 x 36 for plasma and plasma-city), which changes the screen count. plasma-city ran four days later, knowing the other two existed; its worker was fenced off from their work, and its logs were checked for any read of it. The computer thread stepped in three times during plasma-city's run: it restarted the bead store and woke the stopped worker at 23:57Z, and mailed the mayor to start merge rounds 2 and 3.
